# Sprint Retrospective

## Purpose

Reflect on **how** the team worked during the sprint — process, tools, collaboration, communication — and agree on concrete improvements. This is the meeting where the team improves itself, distinct from Sprint Review which is about the product.

## When & Duration

- Last event of the sprint, right after Sprint Review, before the next Sprint Planning.
- ~1.5 hours for a 2-week sprint (roughly 45 minutes per week of sprint).

## Attendees

- **Scrum team only** — PO, Scrum Master, dev team.
- **No outside stakeholders.** This needs to be a safe space for candid feedback about how the team works; external attendees change what people are willing to say.

## How It Should Go

1. **Set the stage** (5 min): brief reminder of the sprint's events/data (velocity, incidents, notable moments) to ground the discussion in facts, not just vibes.
2. **Gather data** (15–20 min): everyone reflects on what happened. Common structures:
   - *Start / Stop / Continue*
   - *Mad / Sad / Glad*
   - *4 Ls: Liked / Learned / Lacked / Longed for*
   Pick one and rotate occasionally to keep it fresh — using the same format every time causes fatigue and shallower input.
3. **Generate insights** (15–20 min): group similar points, discuss root causes, not just symptoms — "why did the deploy fail" not just "the deploy failed."
4. **Decide actions** (15 min): pick a **small number** of concrete, actionable improvements — 1 to 3, not ten. Each needs a clear owner and a way to know it happened.
5. **Close** (5 min): confirm the actions and owners out loud so there's no ambiguity walking out of the room.

## Outcome / Definition of Done for This Meeting

- [ ] 1–3 concrete action items are written down, each with an owner.
- [ ] Action items are added to the next sprint (as a ticket, a standing agenda item, or similar) so they're actually tracked, not just discussed and forgotten.
- [ ] Previous retro's action items were revisited at the start of this one — did they happen? Did they help?
- [ ] Everyone had a chance to speak; the conversation wasn't dominated by one or two people.

## Common Pitfalls

- **No action items, or too many** — a retro that produces zero changes is theater; one that produces ten is a to-do list nobody will do.
- **Never following up on past action items** — the fastest way to make a team stop taking retros seriously.
- **Blame-focused discussion** — the goal is improving the system/process, not identifying who to blame for a mistake.
- **Stakeholders or managers attending** — even with good intentions, their presence changes what the team is willing to say honestly.
